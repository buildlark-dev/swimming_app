# Feature Specification: The swimmer's request

**Feature Branch**: `008-swimmer-request`

**Created**: 2026-11-02

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: User description (translated from the coach's Spanish): "The swimmer's request. A swimmer can ask their coach for a plan: they answer a short list of questions — what they want to work on, how many days they can train, what hurts, which event is coming — and out of that comes a draft that reaches the coach's queue. The draft is not a plan: nobody receives it, nobody swims it, and it does not leave the queue without the coach's signature. He can correct it entirely, reject it saying why, or approve it; the swimmer sees what state their request is in. A swimmer cannot ask on someone else's behalf, and a minor without valid consent cannot send anything off the phone. The visible vocabulary stays in Spanish. The questions are all the ones the engine needs — the coach asked that the master prompt be reviewed to find out which — and the draft is generated by the deterministic engine from feature 002."

> **A note on language.** This specification is written in English because everything
> published to GitHub is. The **visible vocabulary of the app stays in Spanish** and in the
> coach's own notation (`Generar`, `Ver Plan`, `Ajustes`, AL, PN, `NxD / t`, RPE): the
> swimmers are Colombian and some of them are seven years old. Where this document quotes
> what a person sees on screen, it quotes it in Spanish.

## First, the thing that has to be said: a swimmer cannot answer eleven questions

The coach asked for "all the questions that are necessary, according to the master prompt".
Section 5 of `PROMPT_MAESTRO_V3.4` lists eleven mandatory inputs before anything is generated,
and the deterministic engine from feature 002 asks for the same ones. Cross them with who
actually knows each one and the result is not a list of eleven questions:

| Input the engine needs | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Swimmer's category | **Their record.** The coach assigns it; it is not the swimmer's opinion |
| Target stroke | Their record, and the swimmer **confirms or asks for another** |
| Target event | Their record, and the swimmer **confirms or asks for another** |
| Technical diagnosis | **Their record.** These are errors the coach observed. A swimmer does not diagnose themselves |
| ATR phase | **Nobody can ask the swimmer this** |
| Accentuated component | **Nobody can ask the swimmer this** |
| Available days | **The swimmer**, who knows when they can go |
| Session duration | **The swimmer** |
| Pool length | **The swimmer**, who knows where they train |
| Competition date | **The swimmer**, who knows when they compete. A fact, not a judgement |
| Dates with no service | The club. Not the swimmer's to know |
| Injuries or discomfort | **Their recent sessions**, not a new question (clarification Q2) |

So the short list **is short out of necessity, not convenience**: the swimmer answers five
things and confirms two. The rest is already on their record, comes from their sessions, or is
the coach's judgement, and asking them for it would be asking them to have an opinion on what
is not theirs. Principle IV says the coach's judgement is not delegated; this is the most
direct case of that principle since the project began, because here the request **comes from
below**.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-11-02

- **Q: The engine requires an ATR phase when the category uses ATR, forbids it when it does
  not, and validates the accentuated component against it. Neither is the swimmer's to give.
  What happens to them?**
  → **A: the system derives them.** The phase, by counting back from the competition date
  using the block durations section 12 cites (Issurin 2010: Realización 8-15 days,
  Transformación 2-4 weeks, Acumulación 2-6 weeks). The accentuated component, from the phase.

  This has **two consequences the coach needs to know**, written down here so they are not
  discovered in use:

  1. **There is one more question for the swimmer: when they compete.** With no date there is
     no phase to derive. It is a fact they know, not a judgement, so they can be asked — but
     if they do not know it, the system **does not invent a phase**: the request reaches the
     queue with no draft and the phase left for the coach to set (rule R3: when data is
     missing the system asks, it does not assume).
  2. **The derivation is a rule, and this app's rules are audited.** The derived phase is
     written into the draft together with where it came from, so the coach reads
     "Transformación, because the competition is in 24 days" and can change it with one tap.
     A derived phase that cannot be seen or corrected would be judgement delegated through the
     back door.

- **Q: Feature 003 already collects discomfort per session, and this is a minor's health data.
  Is it asked for again here?**
  → **A: it is not asked for.** The request links what the swimmer already reported in their
  recent sessions, and the coach sees it when opening the request. No new health data, no new
  store, and no extra question in a list a nine-year-old has to be able to answer.

  The trade-off, said out loud: **a discomfort that appeared today will not be there if there
  has been no session to report it in.** What the app does not do is pretend otherwise: the
  request says which dates the discomfort it shows came from, and when there is none recent it
  says so, instead of a silence that reads as "nothing hurts".

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - The swimmer asks, and what comes out is a draft (Priority: P1)

A swimmer opens `Generar` — which until now told them "todavía no" — and finds a short list:
what they want to work on, which event is coming, when they compete, which days they can
train, how long each session lasts and in which pool. They answer, send, and the app tells
them their request arrived. **It does not tell them they have a plan**, because they do not.

**Why this priority**: it is the feature. Without it there is nothing: it is the first time in
the product that a swimmer proposes something instead of only receiving and answering.

**Independent Test**: fully testable with a swimmer account: answer the list, send, and check
that the request is recorded and that no new plan appears on any of the swimmer's screens.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a swimmer with a record and valid consent, **When** they answer the list and
   send, **Then** their request is recorded and they see what state it is in
2. **Given** a request just sent, **When** the swimmer walks their destinations, **Then** no
   new plan and no new session of theirs appears
3. **Given** the list half answered, **When** an input the engine needs is missing, **Then**
   the app says which one and does not allow sending
4. **Given** a swimmer with no record, **When** they open `Generar`, **Then** they are told
   their coach has not enrolled them yet, instead of a list that cannot lead anywhere
5. **Given** a swimmer in an ATR category who says when they compete, **When** the draft is
   generated, **Then** the phase comes from counting back from that date, and the draft says
   which phase came out and why
6. **Given** a swimmer who does not know when they compete, **When** they send, **Then** the
   request arrives anyway, with no draft and the phase left for the coach
7. **Given** a swimmer in a category without ATR, **When** the draft is generated, **Then** it
   carries no phase, because the engine forbids one there

---

### User Story 2 - The coach's queue (Priority: P1)

The coach sees the requests that reached him, with who asked and what they asked for, and can
open the draft that came out of it. From there: correct it entirely with the editor that
already exists, reject it **saying why**, or approve it. None of that is automatic.

**Why this priority**: as critical as the first. A request that reaches a queue nobody can
work is not a request, it is a form that goes nowhere.

**Independent Test**: with a seeded request, the coach opens it, rejects it with a reason, and
the swimmer reads the reason.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a request in the queue, **When** the coach opens it, **Then** he sees who asked,
   what they answered and the draft that came out
2. **Given** a request's draft, **When** the coach corrects it, **Then** he uses the same
   editor and the same audit as a plan of his own
3. **Given** a request the coach rejects, **When** he rejects it, **Then** he has to write a
   reason, and without one it cannot be rejected
4. **Given** a rejected request, **When** the swimmer looks at their request, **Then** they
   read the reason their coach wrote
5. **Given** a draft that does not pass the section 16 audit, **When** the coach tries to
   approve it, **Then** he cannot, and he sees which criteria are missing

---

### User Story 3 - The swimmer sees where theirs stands (Priority: P2)

The swimmer sees the state of what they asked for: waiting, under review, approved, or
rejected with its reason. No promised dates and no wording that hints there is something
swimmable before there is.

**Why this priority**: without it the request feels like an envelope dropped in a postbox. But
the feature works without it — the coach can work the queue all the same — so it comes after.

**Independent Test**: move a request through its four states and check the swimmer reads each
one, with no invented dates.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a request in each of its states, **When** the swimmer looks at it, **Then** they
   read which one it is in, in Spanish and without jargon
2. **Given** a waiting request, **When** the swimmer looks at it, **Then** no answer date is
   promised to them

---

### User Story 4 - The minor's gate, here too (Priority: P1)

A minor without valid consent sends nothing off the phone. The list can be answered and what
is answered stays on the device, just like their feature 003 records; the app says the
guardian's authorisation is missing and **does not pretend the request was sent**.

**Why this priority**: it is principle V, and it is the same gate feature 004 built. Leaving it
out here would open a new path for a minor's data to leave without authorisation, which is
exactly what that gate exists to prevent.

**Independent Test**: with a minor without valid consent, answer the list and verify against
the server that nothing arrived, and that the app said so.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a minor without valid consent, **When** they answer the list and send, **Then**
   nothing leaves the device and the app explains the authorisation is missing
2. **Given** that same swimmer, **When** their guardian authorises, **Then** what was held can
   leave without being written again
3. **Given** any swimmer, **When** they try to ask on someone else's behalf, **Then** they
   cannot: a request belongs to whoever sent it and to nobody else

---

### Edge Cases

- **The swimmer already has a request waiting.** They do not pile up: while one is unanswered,
  they do not send another, and they are told why.
- **The swimmer asks for days that do not exist** — seven weekly sessions in Iniciación. The
  engine has per-category ranges and the audit checks them: the draft comes out flagged with
  what does not add up, and it is the coach who decides, not the app.
- **The swimmer carries a discomfort from their recent sessions.** It does not modify the plan.
  The methodology is explicit: it is recorded and flagged, because deducing which exercise is
  contraindicated would be simulating clinical judgement. What changes is that the coach sees
  it when he decides, not afterwards.
- **The swimmer's record is incomplete** — no category, no target event. The engine cannot
  start from there. They are told their coach has to complete their record, instead of
  generating with gaps filled in by eye (rule R3: when data is missing, ask).
- **The coach approves the draft and then changes his mind.** An approved or published plan is
  immutable (FR-025 of feature 001): what exists is a new version, not an edit.
- **Two requests from the same swimmer weeks apart.** The second inherits nothing from the
  first: each one is answered in full.
- **The swimmer stops being one** — their record is erased through data deletion (feature 007).
  Their requests go with what was theirs.
- **A request arrives while the coach has no connection.** The queue is a read: what is in the
  cache is shown, and the app says the latest may be missing.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**The list of questions**

- **FR-001**: The app MUST ask the swimmer only what they know: which days they can train, how
  long each session lasts, which pool length they train in, and when their coming competition
  is
- **FR-002**: The app MUST show them the target stroke and event on their record, and let them
  **confirm or ask for others** for this request
- **FR-003**: The app MUST NOT ask the swimmer for their category, their ATR phase, their
  accentuated component or their technical diagnosis. The first and last come from their
  record; the other two are the coach's judgement
- **FR-004**: The app MUST NOT allow a request to be sent while an input the engine needs is
  missing, and MUST say which one
- **FR-005**: Every question MUST be answerable **without writing free text**: the whole list
  is answered by tapping
- **FR-006**: The swimmer MUST be able to say they **do not know when they compete**, and that
  answer MUST NOT prevent them from sending the request
- **FR-007**: The list MUST be answerable offline, and what is answered MUST survive closing
  the app

**What comes out of the list**

- **FR-008**: The draft MUST be generated by the **deterministic engine from feature 002**,
  with no language model, using the rules already encoded
- **FR-009**: The draft MUST be born in state `borrador`, like any output of automation
  (principle IV)
- **FR-010**: The draft MUST be marked with **how** it was generated and from which request,
  and with the methodology version it was built under
- **FR-011**: The draft MUST pass the section 16 audit before it can be approved, and the app
  MUST show which criteria it meets and which it does not
- **FR-012**: The system MUST NOT deliver, publish or assign the draft to anyone by virtue of
  it having been generated
- **FR-013**: The swimmer MUST NOT be able to see the draft's contents before their coach
  approves it: what they see is the state of their request
- **FR-014**: When the category uses ATR, the system MUST **derive the phase** by counting back
  from the competition date, using the block durations cited in section 12 of the methodology:
  Realización for the last 8-15 days, Transformación for the 2-4 weeks before that,
  Acumulación before that
- **FR-015**: When the category does **not** use ATR, the system MUST NOT set a phase: the
  engine forbids one in that case
- **FR-016**: The system MUST derive the accentuated component from the phase, and the result
  MUST pass the coherence check the engine already performs between phase and component
- **FR-017**: The draft MUST show **which phase was derived and why** — in terms of the
  competition date and the days remaining — and the coach MUST be able to change it
- **FR-018**: With no competition date and a category that uses ATR, the system MUST NOT
  generate a draft: the request MUST reach the queue with the phase pending, for the coach to
  set (rule R3)

**The coach's queue**

- **FR-019**: The coach MUST see the requests that reached him, with who asked, when, what
  they answered and the draft that came out
- **FR-020**: The coach MUST be able to correct the draft with the same editor and the same
  audit as a plan of his own
- **FR-021**: The coach MUST be able to reject a request, and the system MUST require a written
  reason
- **FR-022**: The coach MUST be able to approve the draft, and only him
- **FR-023**: The system MUST NOT move a request between states on its own, neither by elapsed
  time nor by any other automatic rule
- **FR-024**: The queue MUST distinguish what is awaiting an answer from what has been answered

**What the swimmer sees**

- **FR-025**: The swimmer MUST see what state their request is in: waiting, under review,
  approved or rejected
- **FR-026**: If it was rejected, the swimmer MUST read the reason their coach wrote
- **FR-027**: The system MUST NOT promise the swimmer any answer date
- **FR-028**: The visible vocabulary MUST stay in Spanish and in the coach's notation

**Who may ask**

- **FR-029**: A request MUST belong to whoever sent it. A swimmer MUST NOT be able to ask on
  someone else's behalf, nor read someone else's request
- **FR-030**: A minor without valid consent MUST NOT be able to send a request off the device,
  and the app MUST say the guardian's authorisation is missing instead of pretending it was
  sent
- **FR-031**: What was held for lack of consent MUST be able to leave, without being written
  again, as soon as consent exists
- **FR-032**: A swimmer MUST NOT have more than one request awaiting an answer at a time
- **FR-033**: When a swimmer's data is erased (feature 007), their requests MUST go with it

**Discomfort**

- **FR-034**: The request MUST NOT ask about discomfort: it MUST link what the swimmer already
  reported in their recent sessions
- **FR-035**: Discomfort MUST NOT modify the plan the engine generates. The methodology is
  explicit: it is recorded and flagged, because deducing which exercise is contraindicated
  would be simulating clinical judgement
- **FR-036**: Linked discomfort MUST be made visible to the coach when he opens the request,
  without being resolved automatically or hidden behind a generic answer (principle IV)
- **FR-037**: The request MUST say **which dates** the discomfort it shows came from, and MUST
  say explicitly when there is none recent, instead of staying silent: a blank space reads as
  "nothing hurts"
- **FR-038**: The request MUST NOT store a copy of the discomfort: it links it where it already
  lives, so that erasing the swimmer's data takes it away once

### Key Entities

- **Request**: what a swimmer asked for. Carries whose it is, when it was sent, what they
  answered, what state it is in, and the reason if it was rejected. **It does not carry the
  plan**: it carries the reference to the draft that came out of it
- **Request answers**: the inputs the swimmer supplied — days, duration, pool, stroke and event
  confirmed or asked for, competition date. This is what makes the request auditable: what the
  draft was generated from can be seen
- **Request state**: `esperando` → `en revisión` → `aprobada` | `rechazada`. Only the coach
  moves it
- **Derived phase**: the ATR phase the system deduced, **with its reason** — the competition
  date and the days that remained. It travels in the draft so the coach can see it and change
  it. A derived phase that cannot be seen or corrected would be judgement delegated through
  the back door
- **Linked discomfort**: not the request's own. These are the feature 003 reports that fall
  inside the recent window, shown with their dates. The request points at them; it does not
  copy them
- **Generated draft**: a `Plan` in state `borrador`, of the kind that has existed since feature
  001, marked with its origin

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: A nine-year-old swimmer answers the whole list and sends it unaided, in under
  three minutes
- **SC-002**: No request produces content visible to a swimmer without their coach having
  approved it — verifiable in 100% of cases, including a draft that passes the audit first time
- **SC-003**: 100% of drafts generated from a request are born in `borrador` and carry a record
  of how they were generated and under which methodology version
- **SC-004**: Two requests with the same answers and the same record produce the same draft,
  verifiable offline
- **SC-005**: No request from a minor without valid consent leaves the device — verifiable
  against the server, not just on screen
- **SC-006**: The coach goes from seeing a request to having the draft in front of him in fewer
  than two taps
- **SC-007**: 100% of rejections carry a reason written by the coach, and the swimmer reads it
- **SC-008**: No swimmer can read or send another swimmer's request, verified against the
  access rules and not only in the interface
- **SC-009**: 100% of drafts with a derived phase show **which it is and why**, and the coach
  changes it in one tap
- **SC-010**: No draft in a category without ATR carries a phase, and none in a category with
  ATR is generated without one — both sides of the rule, verified
- **SC-011**: The request stores not one copy of a discomfort: what the coach sees comes from
  where it already lived

## Assumptions

- **A request produces one microcycle, no more.** The feature 002 engine generates a week, and
  above the microcycle the domain has nothing: `Macrociclo` and `Mesociclo` arrive in 009.
  Asking for a long plan from here would mean inventing that hierarchy along the way
- **An approved draft follows the lifecycle that already exists** (`borrador` → `aprobado` →
  `publicado`, feature 001). Approving the request does not deliver the plan: delivering it is
  the separate act feature 007 already has in `Ver Plan`. That way the coach can sign today and
  deliver when it suits him
- **Dates with no service are set by the coach**, not the swimmer: they belong to the club. If
  none are declared, the engine works with the days the swimmer gave
- **The technical diagnosis comes from the swimmer's record**, where the coach writes it. If it
  is empty, the standard progression applies and **no errors are invented** (section 7 of the
  master prompt)
- **The category comes from the record.** If it is missing, nothing is generated: the swimmer
  is told their coach has to complete it
- **The swimmer's `Generar` stops saying "todavía no"** and becomes this destination. The
  feature 007 routing already has the slot
- **The coach's queue lives in his `Generar`**, next to the generator and editor already there,
  and not in a new destination: the five destinations from 007 do not change
- **A minor with valid consent asks like anyone else.** The gate looks at consent, not at age
- **The "recent discomfort" window is the last four weeks**, which covers one microcycle plus
  the previous one. It is declared here so it is verifiable, not because the methodology fixes
  that number
- **The competition date belongs to this request, not to the record.** It is not stored on the
  swimmer's profile: a competition that has passed should not keep deciding phases
- **The swimmer cannot withdraw a sent request.** If they change their mind, they tell their
  coach, who rejects it. Adding a withdrawal would open the question of what happens to a draft
  the coach is already correcting

## Dependencies

- **Feature 002** (deterministic engine) — it is what generates the draft. Already in place,
  with its tests
- **Feature 001** (lifecycle and audit) — the draft is born and audited with what already
  exists
- **Feature 004** (consent) — the gate that stops a minor's data leaving without
  authorisation. Already in place
- **Feature 007** (the HUB) — the swimmer's `Generar` destination and the coach's `Generar`
  where the queue lives

## Out of Scope

- Plans longer than one microcycle: that is 009
- The swimmer seeing the plan's contents before the signature
- Any automatic suggestion about a discomfort
- Asking the swimmer about discomfort: it comes from their sessions (clarification Q2)
- A club competition calendar. The date comes from what the swimmer says, and if they do not
  know, the coach sets the phase
- Push notifications that a request arrived or was answered
